Deep Learning

Explore celestial objects through clear explanations, scientific facts, and guided discoveries.

🪐

Planets

Meet the eight planets in our solar system and learn what makes each special.

Discover: rocky worlds, gas giants, icy giants, orbits and moons.

Inside our planetary neighbourhood

Our Solar System formed about 4.6 billion years ago. The four inner planets are rocky worlds; Jupiter and Saturn are gas giants; Uranus and Neptune are ice giants.

  • Inner worlds: Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars.
  • Outer giants: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune.
  • Small bodies: dwarf planets, moons, comets and asteroids.

Earth is one of eight planets orbiting the Sun.

☀️

The Sun

Discover our star, its layers, nuclear fusion, and the energy that supports life on Earth.

Discover: the core, photosphere, corona, sunspots and solar flares.

What happens inside a star?

The Sun is a huge ball of hot plasma. In its core, hydrogen nuclei fuse to form helium and release energy. That energy travels outward and eventually reaches us as sunlight.

  • Core: the fusion energy source.
  • Photosphere: the visible layer.
  • Corona: the very hot outer atmosphere.

Solar images reveal activity invisible to our eyes.

✨

Stars

Learn how stars form, shine, and change throughout their lives.

Discover: nebulae, star colours, stellar life cycles and supernovae.

How stars are born and change

Stars form inside clouds of gas and dust called nebulae. Gravity gathers the material until the centre becomes hot enough for nuclear fusion to begin.

  • Colour: blue stars are generally hotter than red stars.
  • Lifetime: massive stars use their fuel faster.
  • Endings: some become white dwarfs; massive stars may leave neutron stars or black holes.

Nebulae are stellar nurseries filled with gas and dust.

🌌

Galaxy

Explore the Milky Way, its spiral arms, central region, and billions of stars.

Discover: spiral arms, galactic cores, nebulae and dark matter.

Inside a giant star city

Galaxies are enormous systems held together by gravity. They contain stars, planets, gas, dust, stellar remnants and dark matter. The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy.

  • Disc: contains most of its gas, dust and young stars.
  • Spiral arms: regions with gas and star-forming clouds.
  • Halo: surrounds the bright disc and contains dark matter.

Galaxies come in spiral, elliptical and irregular shapes.

🕳️

Black Hole

Understand event horizons, extreme gravity, and how some black holes form.

Discover: accretion discs, light bending and gravitational waves.

What can we observe near one?

A black hole has an event horizon, a boundary beyond which light cannot escape. The black hole itself is dark, but hot gas around it can glow brightly.

  • Event horizon: the point-of-no-return boundary.
  • Accretion disc: hot matter orbiting nearby.
  • Gravity: can bend light from objects behind it.

This is an artist's view, not a photograph of the black hole itself.

💥

Supernova

Learn how some stars explode and spread elements that help build future stars and planets.

Discover: stellar explosions, expanding gas and neutron stars.

A star's dramatic transformation

A supernova is a powerful stellar explosion. In some cases, a massive star's core collapses; another type can happen when a white dwarf undergoes a runaway thermonuclear explosion.

  • Explosion: ejects material into space.
  • Elements: helps spread elements through the galaxy.
  • Remnant: may include a neutron star, black hole or expanding gas.

Supernova remnants keep expanding long after the explosion.

🌠

The Big Bang

Discover how the universe evolved from an early hot, dense state.

Discover: cosmic expansion, the early universe and background radiation.

How the universe evolved

The Big Bang model describes the universe expanding from an early hot, dense state about 13.8 billion years ago. Space itself expanded and cooled, allowing atoms, stars and galaxies to form.

  • Expansion: distant galaxies are generally moving apart.
  • Afterglow: the cosmic microwave background is ancient light from the early universe.
  • Structure: gravity gathered matter into stars and galaxies.

Deep-field images show galaxies from many stages of cosmic history.
